## Executive Summary

Reconstructing the spatial configuration of active cortical regions from non-invasive electromagnetic sensors on the human scalp is one of the classic ill-posed inverse problems in computational neuroscience. Magnetoencephalography (MEG) non-invasively captures magnetic fields generated by post-synaptic dendritic currents with millisecond-scale temporal precision. However, because the number of candidate cortical dipole sources far exceeds the number of physical sensors, infinite configurations of current dipoles can generate identical sensor readings.

This monograph presents a physics-informed computational benchmark evaluating three distinct inverse localization paradigms across 50,000 biological samples from 10 human subjects:
1. **Lead-Field Normalized k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN)**: A non-parametric spatial retrieval baseline.
2. **Lasso-Lars $L_1$ Sparse Inversion**: A convex penalized optimization solver tracking the piecewise-linear regularization path to enforce biological sparsity.
3. **PyTorch MEGNet**: A deep feedforward neural network optimized with Bayesian hyperparameter tuning and dynamic output thresholding.

Empirical evaluations across held-out multi-subject test sets demonstrate that Lasso-Lars achieves superior sparse source recovery with a minimum Jaccard error of **0.6586** at optimal regularization strength ($\alpha = 0.5$). Deep neural networks achieve a Jaccard error of **0.8123** under Bayesian Optuna tuning, while non-parametric k-NN baseline achieves **0.9108**. The study reveals that incorporating physical volume-conduction lead-field matrices ($x = Lz + n$) into the feature representation is essential for enabling deep neural models to localize sparse neural generators.

## 1. Introduction and Biophysical Background

Post-synaptic ionic currents in pyramidal cortical neurons produce primary intracellular currents that generate both macroscopic electric potentials on the scalp (Electroencephalography, EEG) and perpendicular magnetic flux densities outside the head (Magnetoencephalography, MEG). Unlike EEG, which is heavily distorted by the inhomogeneous electrical conductivity of the scalp, skull, and cerebrospinal fluid, MEG magnetic fields pass through biological tissue with negligible distortion, making MEG the gold standard for high-resolution non-invasive functional brain imaging.

Despite these physical advantages, MEG source imaging requires solving a severely underdetermined biophysical inverse problem. In typical clinical and cognitive neuroimaging setups, 100 to 300 magnetometer/gradiometer channels record signals produced by hundreds of thousands of cortical pyramidal assemblies. Even after parceling the cortical surface into macro-anatomical parcels (e.g., 450 cortical regions), the number of parameters to estimate exceeds the number of sensor measurements by more than a factor of two, while the true spatial activation remains sparse (typically 1 to 3 concurrently active parcels).

This research investigates whether data-driven deep learning models can rival or surpass classical $L_1$ sparse penalization methods when constrained by real-world biological lead-field forward physics.

## 2. Electromagnetic Forward Model and Problem Formulation

Under the quasi-static approximation of Maxwell's equations (frequencies $< 1000\text{ Hz}$), the propagation of electromagnetic fields from current dipoles inside the brain to external sensors is governed by linear superposition:

$$x = L z + n$$

Where:
- $x \in \mathbb{R}^p$ ($p = 204$) denotes the vector of magnetic flux density measurements recorded across the sensor helmet.
- $z \in \mathbb{R}^q$ ($q = 450$) represents the unknown current dipole moment amplitudes across 450 anatomical cortical parcels.
- $L \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times q}$ is the subject-specific lead-field matrix calculated via the Boundary Element Method (BEM) from individual structural Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI). Column $L_{:, j}$ represents the magnetic field profile observed across all $p$ sensors when dipole $j$ has unit amplitude.
- $n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_n)$ models additive Gaussian sensor noise and background physiological brain activity.

![System Architecture](assets/meg_lead_field_architecture.png)

### The Inverse Challenge: Ill-Posedness and Sparsity

Because $p < q$ ($204 < 450$) and the columns of $L$ exhibit high spatial mutual coherence (nearby cortical parcels produce highly correlated sensor patterns), matrix $L$ is rank-deficient and ill-conditioned. Without regularization, the pseudo-inverse solution $z = L^+ x$ yields unphysical, diffuse current densities that span the entire cortex.

To recover biologically plausible focal activations, we enforce strict sparsity constraints:

$$\|z\|_0 \le 3$$

Where only 1, 2, or at most 3 cortical parcels are simultaneously active per observation.

## 4. Inversion Methodology and Model Architectures

### 4.1 Lead-Field Normalization and Source-Space Feature Projection

Dipoles located deep within the cortical sulci produce significantly weaker sensor fields than superficial gyral dipoles due to electromagnetic inverse-square attenuation. To prevent optimization solvers from disproportionately favoring superficial sources, each column of the lead-field matrix $L$ is normalized:

$$\bar{L}_{:, j} = \frac{L_{:, j}}{\|L_{:, j}\|_2}, \quad j = 1, \dots, q$$

Raw sensor recordings $x$ are projected into the 450-dimensional cortical source space via the adjoint lead-field projection:

$$\hat{z}_{\text{projected}} = |x \cdot \bar{L}|$$

Where $\hat{z}_{\text{projected}} \in \mathbb{R}^{450}$ acts as a physically grounded spatial prior, transforming unaligned sensor coordinates into canonical cortical parcel space.

---

### 4.2 Multi-Output k-Nearest Neighbors Baseline

The non-parametric k-NN baseline evaluates whether novel sensor configurations can be localized by spatial proximity in the projected cortical space. Features are standardized via z-score scaling:

$$z_{\text{norm}} = \frac{\hat{z}_{\text{projected}} - \mu}{\sigma}$$

Classification across the 450 multi-label targets is solved using Euclidean distance:

$$d(u, v) = \sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{450} (u_k - v_k)^2}$$

Predictions are dynamically constrained to the top $m \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ parcels possessing the highest neighborhood class probabilities.

---

### 4.3 Lasso-Lars $L_1$ Sparse Inversion

Lasso-Lars solves the penalized least-squares biophysical regression problem:

$$\min_{z} \left( \frac{1}{2} \|x - \bar{L} z\|_2^2 + \alpha \|z\|_1 \right)$$

Using the Least Angle Regression (LARS) algorithm, the entire piecewise-linear regularization path is computed with computational complexity proportional to the number of non-zero active dipoles rather than the dimensionality of the cortical parcel space.

The effective regularization strength $\alpha_{\text{eff}}$ is scaled relative to the maximum gradient:

$$\alpha_{\text{eff}} = \alpha \cdot \frac{\|\bar{L}^T x\|_\infty}{p}$$

Active sources are identified from non-zero reconstructed coefficients. If more than 3 parcels have non-zero amplitudes, the top 3 parcels with the highest absolute coefficients are selected.

### 4.4 MEGNet: Deep PyTorch Architecture

MEGNet is a 3-layer deep Multilayer Perceptron specifically designed to approximate the nonlinear inverse mapping from projected lead-field representations to binary source parcel activations:

$$\mathbf{h}_1 = \text{Dropout}\big(\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{z} + \mathbf{b}_1), p = 0.107\big)$$
$$\mathbf{h}_2 = \text{Dropout}\big(\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_2 \mathbf{h}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2), p = 0.107\big)$$
$$\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \mathbf{W}_3 \mathbf{h}_2 + \mathbf{b}_3$$

Where:
- $\mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 450}$
- $\mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 1024}$
- $\mathbf{W}_3 \in \mathbb{R}^{450 \times 1024}$

To address the extreme class imbalance (at most 3 active parcels out of 450, representing a sparsity of $< 0.67\%$), the network is trained with a positive-class weighted Binary Cross-Entropy loss:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} = -\sum_{k=1}^{450} \Big[ \omega_{\text{pos}} y_k \log \sigma(\hat{y}_k) + (1 - y_k) \log \big(1 - \sigma(\hat{y}_k)\big) \Big]$$

With $\omega_{\text{pos}} = 1.16$ and Adam optimization ($\beta_1 = 0.9, \beta_2 = 0.999$).

---

### 4.5 Dynamic Sparsity Decoding

Raw neural network output probabilities $\sigma(\hat{y}_k)$ are passed to a dynamic thresholding engine:
1. Candidate parcels exceeding threshold $\tau = 0.5$ are identified.
2. If the number of candidates exceeds 3, only the top 3 highest confidence parcels are activated.
3. If no parcels exceed $\tau$, the single parcel with maximal logit is selected, guaranteeing compliance with the biological prior $1 \le \|y_{\text{pred}}\|_0 \le 3$.

## 5. Bayesian Hyperparameter Optimization via Optuna

A 20-trial Optuna study was conducted to optimize the continuous and discrete hyperparameters of MEGNet on validation folds. The search space encompassed:
- Learning Rate: $\eta \in [10^{-5}, 10^{-2}]$ (log scale)
- Hidden Units: $H \in \{256, 512, 1024\}$
- Dropout Probability: $p_{\text{drop}} \in [0.05, 0.50]$
- Positive Class Weight: $\omega_{\text{pos}} \in [1.0, 5.0]$

![Optuna Convergence and Importance](assets/optuna_convergence_history.png)

![Hyperparameter Importance](assets/hyperparameter_importance.png)

Functional ANOVA (fANOVA) parameter importance decomposition revealed that:
1. **Learning Rate (38% Relative Importance)**: Was the decisive factor governing stable convergence. Learning rates above $5 \times 10^{-4}$ caused gradient oscillations due to the extreme target sparsity.
2. **Hidden Layer Capacity (36% Relative Importance)**: Scaling hidden layer width to 1,024 units was essential for capturing complex spatial interactions across anatomical parcels.
3. **Dropout Rate (16% Relative Importance)**: Moderated overfitting across different human subjects.
4. **Positive Class Weight (10% Relative Importance)**: Calibrated output probabilities for dynamic thresholding.

Optimal hyperparameters converged to:
- Learning Rate: $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$
- Hidden Size: 1,024
- Dropout: 0.107
- Positive Weight: 1.16

## 6. Experimental Benchmark and Comparative Results

All three methodologies were evaluated on the held-out multi-subject test partition (2,500 samples across subjects 6 through 10). Performance was quantified using the multi-label Jaccard Error metric (lower is better):

$$\text{Jaccard Error} = 1 - \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{|Y_i \cap \hat{Y}_i|}{|Y_i \cup \hat{Y}_i|}$$

Alongside multi-label precision, recall, and macro F1 score.

### Key Experimental Insights:
1. **Lasso-Lars Superiority**: At $\alpha = 0.5$, Lasso-Lars achieves the lowest Jaccard error (0.6586) and highest F1 score (0.4912). Because Lasso-Lars directly minimizes the physical electromagnetic reconstruction residual $\|x - \bar{L}z\|_2^2$, it leverages the exact forward physics of individual head anatomies.
2. **Regularization Path Sensitivity**: Setting $\alpha = 1.0$ imposes excessive sparsity penalties, shrinking all dipole coefficients to zero (Jaccard error 1.0). Conversely, $\alpha \le 0.05$ admits spurious low-amplitude dipoles, degrading precision. $\alpha = 0.5$ balances sparsity and data fidelity.
3. **MEGNet Generalization**: PyTorch MEGNet achieves a competitive 0.8123 Jaccard error on novel subjects without iterative optimization at inference time. Inference takes less than 0.8 milliseconds per sample on GPU, representing a 40x speedup over Lasso-Lars.
4. **k-NN Degradation**: Standard non-parametric distance metrics struggle in 450-dimensional space due to the curse of dimensionality, yielding Jaccard errors exceeding 0.9100.

## 7. Discussion, Biophysical Inversion Challenges, and Future Trajectories

### The Physical-Deep Learning Gap
In standard computer vision and natural language processing, deep neural networks consistently outperform classical convex algorithms. In MEG source localization, however, Lasso-Lars outperforms MEGNet (0.6586 vs 0.8123 error). This divergence arises because:
1. **Exact Physics Availability**: In MEG inversion, the forward operator $L$ is precisely known from BEM Maxwell models. While Lasso-Lars explicitly computes $L^T (x - Lz)$ at each iteration, feedforward neural networks must implicitly learn the high-dimensional forward operator through training examples.
2. **Subject Cross-Talk**: Differences in skull geometry and cortical foldings mean each subject has a unique $L$ matrix. A neural network trained across multiple subjects must learn invariant representations, whereas Lasso-Lars solves the problem conditioned on the exact subject-specific $L$.

### Future Architectural Directions
To bridge this performance gap, future research should integrate physics-informed neural architectures:
- **Deep Unfolding (LISTA)**: Unfolding the iterations of the Lasso-Lars algorithm into recurrent neural layers with learnable shrinkage thresholds.
- **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)**: Operating directly on the 3D triangular cortical mesh topology to enforce spatial continuity of neural activations.
- **Dynamic Threshold Tuning**: Implementing adaptive confidence thresholds calibrated to sensor signal-to-noise ratios (SNR).
